# T4: weighted endpoint kernels and the opposite exchange

This method covers thirteen **source orbits**, each retaining its own native
momentum/C/J record and its seven Laurent positions. The empty-edge set is
13/14/15/33/34/35/40/43; the opposite-exchange set is 27/28/29/41/42. These are
physical-I relations, not thirteen identical ordered integrals. The program is
[t4.py](../scripts/t4.py); the new exact endpoint algebra is
[t4_endpoints.py](../scripts/t4_endpoints.py). All statements below concern
fixed positive real z and meromorphic continuation in epsilon. Formal status
remains `not_run`.

The package performs a fresh exact inversion and absolute Laurent convolution
for the eight empty-edge orbits, from the originally evaluated raw Q jets. It
also assembles 41/42 from the established bare-T4 and H0 jets. For 27/28 it
reparses the **complete original global-IBP HyperInt outputs**, retaining their
individual finite coefficients. That part reuses an existing integration; the
portable replay does not independently reintegrate those hard kernels.
Generation reads no selected result JSON, old representation table, or current
result selector, and launches no original writer or CAS process.

## From the ordered source to the common kernel

Let e=epsilon, kappa=1-2e, ell=log(z), and
G3=exp(-3 EulerGamma e) Gamma(1-e) Gamma(1-2e). In the D frame the common
three-gluon kernel is

\[
S=\alpha_1\alpha_2y(1-x)
 +\alpha_1\alpha_3\{(1-x)(1-w)+zxw\}
 +z\alpha_2\alpha_3w(1-y),
\]
\[
P=\frac14\left\{
\alpha_1\alpha_2[-(1+z)(1-x)+zy]
+\alpha_1\alpha_3[1+3z-(1+z)x+z(1+z)w]
+z\alpha_2\alpha_3[1-y-(1+z)w]\right\}.
\]

The simplex measure is
\(d\mu_e=d^3\alpha\,\delta(1-\sum\alpha_i)
(\alpha_1\alpha_2\alpha_3)^{-e}\). The original F-frame prepared polynomial
satisfies \(S_F(z)=zS_D(1/z)\), \(P_F(z)=z^2P_D(1/z)\). The per-orbit ordered
domains and these factors must be transported together.

The native C has one Euler compensation and orbit multiplicity four. Its
physical normalization is

\[
I_g=\frac{\kappa G_3}{8}
 \frac{J_g(z,e)+z^{3e}J_g(1/z,e)}{1+z^{3e}}.
\]

The package checks the native assembly sign is +1, the Euler count is one, and
the native raw J agrees with the frozen historical source root. The historical
prepared file serves only as a source bridge; it supplies no alternate sign
branch. Full color reconstruction remains upstream of selecting a1. A kernel,
face, Q coefficient, or product of two physical I values cannot silently replace
the J in this equation.

## Empty-edge orbits: the subintegrals that were actually computed

Integrating the two pure endpoints in their original order produces
\(J_g^F(z,e)=z^{2e}K_g(1/z,e)/e^2\), where
\(K_g=\int d\mu_e\,dx\,dy\,dw\,P S^{-2+2e}W_g\). The four weights are

| Representative | Weight W |
|---|---|
| 13 | \((1-y)^e(1-w^e)\) |
| 14 | \([1-(1-y)^e]\,(1-w^e)\) |
| 15 | \([1-(1-y)^e]w^e\) |
| 40 | \(1-x^e\) |

The original reflection of all Wilson endpoints and alpha1/alpha3 gives
\(J_{35}(z)=z^{3e}J_{13}(1/z)\), and likewise 34/14, 33/15, 43/40. Hence each
pair has equal full I at fixed z. The factor z^(3e) and the transformed ordered
domains are part of the relation; equality of the J integrands is not asserted.

The numerator identity which drives the reduction is

\[
P=\frac{-zS_x-(1+z)S_y+S_w}{4}+R,\qquad
R=\frac{1+z}{2}\alpha_3
 [\alpha_1(1-x+zw)-z\alpha_2w].
\]

Writing B[v,b] for the weighted S^(-kappa) face, and R[W] for the integral
of R W S^(-2+2e), the complete weighted integration by parts is

\[
4\kappa K[W]=z(B_{x,1}-B_{x,0})
+(1+z)(B_{y,1}-B_{y,0})-(B_{w,1}-B_{w,0})
+\int[-zW_x-(1+z)W_y+W_w]S^{-\kappa}
+4\kappa R[W].
\]

The face values are taken first in the initial convergence strip. Replacing
u^e by one at a face would lose genuine poles. For 13/14/15 the w-face and
W_w contribution were integrated as their joint kernel
\(T_w[W]=\int W S_wS^{-2+2e}\), since their sum is kappa T_w. Recoil and
every original face are retained. This is the reason the saved raw Q packets
contain more than a cusp approximation.

The evaluated primitive packets are: the finite Euler kernels F0/F1/G1 through
e^4; the y=0,1 face remainders through e^3; the nondegenerate hard-face moments
through e^2; the recoil moments through e^2 after explicit Euler pole extraction;
and the required derivative/Tw packets. Their original assembly is preserved in
[assemble.py](../snapshots/workflow/checks/tgv-t4-empty/assemble.py) and the
[original mathematical method](../snapshots/benchmarks/tgv/families/t4-empty/method.md).
The source record explicitly lists each of its own raw Q inputs.

For example, the finite Euler integrals are

\[
F_A(e)=\int_0^1db\,du\,[b(1-b)]^{-e}u^{Ae}(1-bu)^{-1+2e},
\quad
G_A(e)=\int_0^1db\,du\,[b(1-b)]^{-e}(1-u)^{Ae}(1-bu)^{-1+2e}.
\]

The hard x=0 face uses alpha=(r,s,1)/(1+r+s), y=u/(1+u), w=v/(1+v),
with denominator \(F_H=r(1+u)+rsu(1+v)+zsv\). Its locally integrable e^0,
e^1,e^2 logarithmic moments were integrated in r,u,s,v order. Recoil and Tw
require Euler extraction before setting e=0: the recoil region {r,1/u} has
degree (1+A)e, and Tw regions {r,v},{v,1/t} have degrees (1+B)e,(2+B)e. The
original runs kept HyperInt divergence checks active. The new Python replay
uses their admitted integrated coefficients, rather than pretending that these
primitive integrations are being repeated.

## The corrected absolute Laurent budget

Set Q_g=4 kappa K_g. The integrated raw coefficients
\(q_{g,k}=[e^k]Q_g\), k=-2,...,2, are read directly from the
[original Q output run](../snapshots/workflow/checks/tgv-t4-empty/runs/20260918T023134199721Z-assemble_13_14_15_40/assemble_13_14_15_40.mpl).
No corrected physical-I coefficient is used as an input. The physical bridge is

\[
I_g=\frac{1}{32e^2}\,p(e,z)\,\mathcal E_g(e,z),\quad
\mathcal E_g=\tfrac12\{z^{-e/2}Q_g(z,e)+z^{e/2}Q_g(1/z,e)\},
\]
\[
p=1+\left(\frac52\zeta_2-\frac98\ell^2\right)e^2
+3\zeta_3e^3
+\left(\frac{193}{40}\zeta_2^2-\frac{45}{16}\zeta_2\ell^2
+\frac{135}{128}\ell^4\right)e^4+O(e^5).
\]

The coefficients are constructed by the absolute-index sums

\[
E_k=\frac12\sum_{j=0}^{k+2}\frac{
(-\ell/2)^j q_{k-j}(z)+(\ell/2)^j q_{k-j}(1/z)}{j!},
\quad -2\le k\le2,
\]
\[
c_r=\frac1{32}\sum_{j=0}^{r+4}p_j E_{r+2-j},
\quad -4\le r\le0,\qquad c_{-6}=c_{-5}=0.
\]

The historical call `series(evenQ,eps=0,3)` used a relative Laurent order.
Since Q can start at e^-2, that call discarded terms needed after the external
e^-2 shift. It changed c_-1 and c_0 for 13/14/15 and their partners 35/34/33.
40/43 have a different leading order and were unaffected. The correction is
not an added finite counterterm: the replay keeps the needed absolute powers
through E_2, and p through e^4, before multiplying. The preserved original
[budget-correction script](../snapshots/workflow/checks/recursion-5e-remainder/t4-budget.mpl)
provides the historical witness. Those corrected I outputs are reserved for
comparison after candidate generation.

The new inversion has an independent, explicit construction. For every 0,-1
word, the constant at positive infinity is obtained by z=u/(1-u). Its kernels
become du/u-du/(u-1) for letter 0, and -du/(u-1) for letter -1. Shuffle
regularization at u=1 sets the one-letter endpoint logarithms to zero. Admissible
0,1 words give signed standard MZVs. Through weight six their values are solved
using exact rational shuffle/stuffle relations, duality, and the depth-one zeta
values; the system has no unresolved coordinate. The invalid product of two
divergent zeta(1) values is not used.

The differential kernels for f(1/z) are -dz/z at head 0 and
(-dz/z+dz/(1+z)) at head -1. Combining their primitives with the just-computed
regularized-infinity constants fixes inversion without fitting any I result.
The program checks inversion twice returns every raw Q, and inversion fixes
every assembled physical coefficient. These algebraic checks are useful
controls of the new replay; they do not independently prove the original
primitive integrations.

## Opposite exchange: complete boundaries before expansion

Let \(D_z(p,q)=(1-p)(1-q)+zpq\). The original ordered regions are x<p,w<q
for 27; x<p,q<w for 28; and p<x,q<w for 29. Their D-frame source is

\[
\widehat J_g=-(1+z)\int_{\mathcal D_g}d\mu_e\,dx\,dy\,dw\,dp\,dq\,
 P S^{-2+2e}D_z(p,q)^{-1+e}.
\]

Its relation to prepared J includes z^(3e) and the reciprocal argument. The
reflection gives I29=I27; 28 has its own self-crossing ordered kernel. The
complete derivation is preserved in the
[opposite method](../snapshots/benchmarks/tgv/families/t4-opposite/method.md).

Integrating alpha and y first leaves an endpoint mother H_e(x,w), its recoil
R_e(x,w), the y-face h_j(x,w;e), and a pure rectangular weight W_g(x,w;e).
Global IBP transfers x,w derivatives to W **with** both endpoint faces.
The 27 w=0 face contains the cusp contribution

\[
B_{27}^{\rm cusp}=\frac{F_0(e)}{2e^2}\mathcal B(e,z),\qquad
\mathcal B=\int_0^1dp\,dq\,D_z(p,q)^{-1+e}
 [1-(1-p)^{2e}]=e b_1+e^2b_2+O(e^3).
\]

It contributes to the simple pole and finite term. The earlier fixed-endpoint
expansion which lost this face is not used. Recoil is Euler-regularized in the
same {r,1/u} region as the bare T4 because the pure factor is independent of
those variables. Hard bulk, x face, reflected w face, y contacts and cusp are
all integrated in the preserved completed
[global-IBP run](../snapshots/workflow/checks/tgv-t4-opposite/runs/20260918T034029006332Z-global_ibp/global_ibp.mpl).

Writing \(\widehat J=\mathcal V/(4\kappa)\),
\(\mathcal V=v_{-1}/e+v_0+O(e)\), its physical coefficients are

\[
c_{-1}=\frac{v_{-1}(z)+v_{-1}(1/z)}{64},\quad
c_0=\frac{v_0(z)+v_0(1/z)}{64}
+\frac{3\ell}{128}[v_{-1}(1/z)-v_{-1}(z)].
\]

The saved full I27m1/I27zero and I28m1/I28zero texts are this original run's
integrated output, not selected JSON tables. This package reparses those four
outputs, checks weights, preserves every exact coordinate, and separately
checks the Omega-sector relation. This is an explicit reuse boundary. The
primitive outputs and scripts remain available for a future cold re-integration.

## Why Omega6 and the special letters must be retained

The original [Omega construction](../snapshots/workflow/checks/tgv-t4-opposite/build_omega.py)
isolates an actual -1/2 leading-letter sector of I28 and integrates a known
weight-five kernel. Its completed
[raw output](../snapshots/workflow/checks/tgv-t4-opposite/runs/20260918T034133130688Z-omega_basis/omega_basis.mpl)
defines

\[
T_6(z)=\int_0^z\frac{\Phi_5(t)}{t+1/2}\,dt,\quad T_6(0)=0,
\qquad \Omega_6(z)=T_6(z)+T_6(1/z).
\]

The output data include the **full** Hlog vectors of Phi5, T6 and Omega6.
Differentiating T6 strips its -1/2 first letter, giving exactly Phi5/(z+1/2).
All T6 words are regular at zero; the boundary is specified, not an unknown
integration constant. Omega6 contains the word (-1/2,0,0,0,0,0) with coefficient
8, and also letters -2. Its periods include log(2) and the explicitly defined
alternating sums

\[
A_4=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(-1)^nH_{n-1}}{n^3}
 =\frac12\int_0^1\frac{\log(1/t)\log^2(1+t)}t\,dt,
\quad
A_6=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(-1)^nH_{n-1}}{n^5}
 =\frac1{12}\int_0^1\frac{\log^3(1/t)\log^2(1+t)}t\,dt.
\]

These are HyperInt's small-index-first zeta[1,-3] and zeta[1,-5]. They are
defined real periods, not numerical fits or unset boundary constants. The
coordinate schema always records six powers in the order zeta2,zeta3,zeta5,
log2,A4,A6, and stores -1/2 as an exact rational string.

Individually c27,0 and c29,0 contain -Omega6/64, while c28,0 contains
+Omega6/32. Subtracting these explicit terms leaves full 0,-1 Hlog expressions
with the original zeta periods. The program keeps both the complete individual
coefficient and its named decomposition. The cancellation in 2c27,0+c28,0 is
a separately checked exact relation; it is not evidence sufficient to validate
any individual integral, and no special coordinate is discarded during
generation. The positive-real branch is the one inherited from the source
integral and this convergent definition. No complex-z/i0 extension is asserted.

## 41/42: complementary domains give their own product factor

The two opposite-exchange positions on edge 2 partition the full square. At
the J level, J41+J42=J_T4 J_pure and J42(z)=z^(3e)J41(1/z). The resulting
individual physical I is

\[
I_{41}=I_{42}=\frac{e^{-\gamma_Ee}\Gamma(1-e)}8
 \frac{\cosh(e\ell)}{\cosh(3e\ell/2)} I_{T4}(z,e)H_0(e,z).
\]

In particular the factor is 1/8 for each orbit after its own reflection and
normalization. Multiplying two physical I values would give a different factor.
The accepted bare-T4 and single-exchange jets are

\[
I_{T4}=\frac{\zeta_2}{8e^2}+\frac{\zeta_3-M_{1,1}}{8e}
-\frac{M_{2,1}}8+\frac{M_{0,1}^2}{48}+\frac9{10}\zeta_2^2+O(e),
\]
\[
H_0=M_{0,1}+\tfrac e2M_{1,1}
+e^2(M_{2,1}/8-M_{0,1}^2/24)+O(e^3).
\]

The remaining analytic factor is
\(1+e^2(5M_{0,1}/4+17\zeta_2/4)+O(e^3)\), with no linear term. The
M generators are read from their own
[original evaluated moment run](../snapshots/workflow/checks/single-opposite/runs/20260916T090538777022Z/compute_moments.mpl),
and their full Hlog definitions are embedded in the generated entry. Thus the
finite product is completely evaluated, with no implicit subintegral. The
established bare-T4 formula is an admitted lower-loop result, rather than a
fresh independent lower-loop integration or a read of its selected I table.

All thirteen entries include their native source binding, actual primitive
inputs, exact dependency hashes, physical-I bridge, own relation scope, epsilon
budget and seven full coefficient vectors. Every nonzero coordinate has weight
6+k at epsilon^k. After generation the integrator freezes the candidate and
only then opens the old tables for comparison. Agreement is exact algebraic
regression evidence. It does not turn reused primitive integration, numerical
source probes, or this calculation-side endpoint algebra into a Lean proof.
